Don’t Know What You’re Doing in IT? Same. And That’s Okay

"Sometimes I stare at my code, my lab instructions, or the blinking lights on a router and just think… what am I even doing?"

If you’ve ever sat in class, completely confused while everyone else seems to get it—hi, you’re not alone. I’ve been there. Still am, honestly. When I started IT, I didn’t have a clear plan. I just… enrolled. No dream job in mind, no tech idol to follow. Just vibes.

At first, it felt like I was blindly walking through a maze of acronyms—IP, OSPF, SQL, IoT, RIP (me). There were times I felt like I didn’t belong. Like I wasn’t techy enough, not smart enough, not whatever enough to be here

But here’s the truth: you don’t have to know everything right away. Most of us are figuring it out as we go. IT is such a wide field. Some people love programming. Others love tinkering with hardware. Some love design, data, cybersecurity—you don’t have to be good at everything. You’ll find your thing eventually.

And the best part? You can fail a few times and still make it. You can feel completely lost one week, and suddenly boom, it clicks. You don’t have to be a genius. You just need to stay curious, keep showing up, and try again.

So if you feel like you’re just winging it—welcome to the club. We’re all just learning, struggling, laughing, Googling error messages, and growing together.

Don’t rush the process. Don’t pressure yourself to be perfect. You’re doing better than you think.

Final Thoughts

You don’t need to have everything figured out right now. It’s okay to feel lost—what matters is that you keep going. Be patient with yourself, stay curious, and allow yourself to grow at your own pace. Remember: you’re not falling behind, you’re just finding your way.
