PERSONAL REFLECTION

From ‘Nope’ to IoT Hope

"I used to panic at the sight of wires and resistors. Now I get excited connecting a Raspberry Pi to an Arduino and accessing it through the web—who would've thought?"

So, here's the thing: IT wasn't exactly my dream major. I woke up one day enrolled, and honestly, I'm still not entirely sure how that happened. It felt like a cosmic joke, a plot twist in the grand narrative of my life. But hey, I'm passing, and that's a victory in itself! And surprisingly, I'm starting to love the challenge.

My biggest nemesis? Hardware. I'm ridiculously clumsy. Give me a breadboard and some wires, and I'll probably accidentally invent a new kind of firework. Ask anyone in my class—they'll have a hilarious story about my hardware mishaps.

Second year rolled around, and we faced a fork in the road: IoT or databases. The advice? "Love SQL? Databases. Love Flutter? IoT." I was lukewarm on both, but the thought of accidentally nuking my entire database with one rogue command? Hard pass. IoT it was. It felt like climbing Mount Improbable, and I was up for the challenge.

IoT 1 was a breeze—mostly Python and a touch of Packet Tracer. Chill vibes only. Then came IoT 2, and I braced myself for the inevitable disaster. Spoiler alert: I was spectacularly wrong. Arduino projects stole my heart. I transformed from a reluctant student into a lab rat, happily gnawing on the delicious cheese of embedded systems.

In IoT 3, lab sessions are my happy place. They call it 'learning with fun,' and I can see why; it doesn’t feel like studying at all. We’re just playing around with circuits and code, and time flies by. Honestly, there are times when I don’t even want to go home!

The best part? We're now connecting Raspberry Pis to Arduinos and accessing them via the web. Mind. Blown. Seeing my progress is the ultimate reward, fueling my excitement for what's next. Who knew clumsy, spark-happy me would conquer the hardware beast? I'm here, wires, codes, and all—and loving every chaotic, exhilarating minute of it.

Final Thoughts

If there’s one thing I’ve learned from this journey, it’s this: don’t pressure yourself when things don’t work right away. It’s okay to make mistakes, get confused, or even start over multiple times. What matters most is that you’re learning and finding joy in the process. Take your time, enjoy the ride, and don’t lose hope—because sometimes, the things that once scared you end up becoming the things you’re most passionate about.
